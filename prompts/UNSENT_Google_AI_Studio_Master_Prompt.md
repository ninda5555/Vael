# Build UNSENT · Google AI Studio master prompt

Build a complete, mobile-first, installable web app called **UNSENT** in Google AI Studio Build. Act as the product designer, multilingual content designer, full-stack engineer, and release engineer. Create and run the application in the project. The deliverable is working source, a usable preview, a tested Firebase backend, and reproducible deployment instructions. Use this document as the complete brief. Where the project already contains working code, inspect it and extend it. Keep one source of truth for each backend operation.

## 1 · The first minute

UNSENT is a creative space for feelings. A young person in West Bengal opens it late at night with something they want to say. Within two taps they can write to someone, turn a feeling into art, or borrow a question from their zodiac sign. A person who has never heard of a Rashi must feel equally welcome.

Design around this single loop: **find a thought → write something true → decide what happens to it**. The three parts are Letters, Feeling Cards, and Dreamer’s Almanac. The user owns the final choice to keep, share, or release. Launch directly into Today with a visible “Write a letter” button. Ask for sign-in only when someone chooses a cloud feature. Avoid a permission carousel, a compulsory birth form, streak pressure, ad trackers, and automatically posted material.

Build the production paths for the integrations below. A first-time developer must also be able to run private journaling and the sign library without entering a credit card. Develop with emulators and synthetic writing. Leave purchasing, upgrading billing, and deploying to a real production project to the project owner.

## 2 · What people actually do with the zodiac

Make **Zodiac** a main tab. Open it to an illustrated library of all twelve signs with recognizable glyphs, English and Rashi names, and a searchable accessible list. Offer a wheel as an alternative navigation view with the same destinations. Tapping a sign should lead to a usable question and a two-minute activity. Give every sign a different visual identity through a motif, paper color, and subtle movement. Keep type contrast high across all twelve. Glyph and text must remain readable with motion off.

Use these original launch seeds. In the detail view show the traditional element, modality and Vedic ruler as cultural reference. The paired names are vocabulary. A person's Western Sun sign and Vedic Moon sign can differ. Never infer either one from the other.

Implement daily use beyond a sign encyclopedia:

1. **Choose my lens.** Let people browse any sign, choose a creative lens manually, calculate a Western tropical Sun sign, calculate a Vedic Moon Rashi using Lahiri, or skip. Make labels visible wherever results appear, for example “Chosen lens · Pisces,” “Birth Sun · Leo,” and “Birth Moon · awaiting birth time.” Only display actual results. Permit browsing a different sign without overwriting the saved profile.
2. **A sign for today.** Each detail page offers a writing spark, the action, a blank-letter shortcut, and “Make a sign card.” Build a reviewed local catalog with at least seven distinct prompts per sign, including friendship, creativity, rest, boundaries, gratitude, curiosity, and starting again. Rotate predictably by selected sign and local date. Changing UI language must translate the same prompt. “Another spark” cycles without duplicates until exhausted.
3. **Dreamer’s Almanac.** A small monthly calendar shows Moon phases and local dates for the next new and full Moon. Tap a date to see the sky fact, an editorial writing invitation, and any private entries the user explicitly linked to that date. “Emotional weather” comes from the user's own check-in or a clearly labeled creative suggestion. Never infer their mood from the Moon. For an offline visitor, reuse valid versioned data for that date or compute locally.
4. **Two-sign conversation cards.** Choose two signs manually and a context such as friendship, family, or creative collaboration. Give each person a question, then suggest a shared activity. Example for Gemini and Pisces: “Describe the same memory twice, once as a headline and once as a color.” Allow identical signs. No birth details about another person, ranking of relationships, percentages, marriage verdicts, or sign-based instructions about important life decisions.
5. **My sky card.** Preview a 9:16 card using a chosen sign motif, an editable line, and optional Moon phase for a clearly identified date. All personal fields start hidden. A birth date, exact time, coordinates, account name and private journal body must never be implicit export fields.

## 3 · Screens and interactions

**Today.** On first visit, show a paper envelope, a primary writing action, a smaller “Make a feeling card” action, and one almanac preview. For return visits offer “Continue draft” only after the vault unlocks. Show dates in the user’s chosen timezone. Use anonymous artwork on locked surfaces.

**Write and seal.** Offer Ex, Mother, Father, Friend, Someone I lost, My past self, My future self, Myself right now, and a custom recipient. Seed at least five prompts for each category. The editable opening “Dear …,” must work in the chosen language. Use a spacious writing area, unobtrusive word count, private draft autosave with visible status, and recovery after refresh. A storage failure leaves the editor usable. Optional voice input is permission-based. Prefer supported on-device dictation. Where browser speech processing may send audio to a provider, explain that before enabling it. Offer typing everywhere. Never request microphone access on launch.

Offer six live paper previews: Old Parchment, Midnight Blue, Rose Petal, Rain on Glass, Torn Notebook, and Moonlit Ink. Sealing applies a dated “NEVER SENT” ink stamp. Support long letters, paragraph breaks, mixed scripts, and returning to edit before sealing. Keep, export, or release are separate explicit actions. On release let the user choose ash, embers, or paper birds, confirm which copies will be removed, and honor reduced motion. A cloud deletion that is pending offline must stay visibly pending across restart. Show completion when the selected copies are gone from active app storage. Explain the scope for backups and previously exported copies.

**Feeling Cards.** Accept a short feeling, an optional self-selected mood, and a visual style. Generate an editable card locally from the user’s own words or a reviewed template. Keep optional AI poem generation behind separate consent. Provide a preview, font size controls, a safe crop area, and support for Bengali and Hindi shaping. Export a 1080 × 1920 PNG on device. For long letters offer passage selection or multiple numbered cards. Never silently truncate text or shrink it to illegibility. Use the system share sheet when file sharing is supported and a download fallback otherwise. Sharing opens a preview first. Hide recipient by default, allow optional subtle UNSENT watermark, and distinguish opening a share sheet from completing a share.

**My Box.** Private archive with Letters, Cards, Favorites, and on-device search after unlock. Open and reread, duplicate as a new draft, delete, or export. A monthly and annual “My year in feelings” recap uses only explicitly recorded check-ins and locally derived counts. Show examples in a separate, removable demo area. Keep example entries out of real recaps. Empty, locked, offline, pending, conflicted, permission-denied, and restored states each need an action that works.

**Settings and privacy.** One place for English, বাংলা, हिन्दी, and Hinglish; Daylight Paper or Midnight; text size; reduced motion; chosen timezone; zodiac preferences; cloud backup status; recovery; consent choices; export; local deletion and account deletion. Explain what leaves the device beside each feature. Keep all visible UI, permission prompts, errors, share previews, and support text localized. Use local font assets with rights to distribute, correct fallback fonts, and real sentence translations. Preserve the user's original writing when switching languages.

## 4 · Visual design that fits a phone

Design for a 390 px Android phone first, then small phones, tablets and desktop. A thumb-reachable bar has Today, Zodiac, Create and My Box with text labels. Create offers Letter or Feeling Card. Today should open in a usable, quiet state within the first viewport. On desktop, keep an editorial reading width with room beside it for the sign print or paper preview. Make the work feel tactile: an imperfect ink impression, page grain, a soft envelope fold, distinct illustrated sign prints. Use optimized original assets and lazy-load the Zodiac gallery. No autoplay video or motion over body text.

Use ink #0E0B1A, midnight #1B1740, dusk #3A2F6B, rose #E8B4B8, candle gold #D9B26F, paper #F5EAD6, and stamp red #B3202A as tokens. Choose Cormorant Garamond for display, Inter for controls, and tested Bengali/Devanagari faces. Use handwriting sparingly. Target WCAG 2.2 AA, 44 px touch targets, logical keyboard order, visible focus, screen-reader form errors, readable 200% zoom, and full operation with reduced motion. Honor the phone keyboard, safe areas and slower connections. Design the unlocked detail, locked vault, first visit, denied permission and offline screens with the same care.

## 6 · Implement the zodiac calculation and MCP contracts

**Astronomy.** For the tropical Sun and the current lunar phase, use a pinned, appropriately licensed deterministic ephemeris such as Astronomy Engine. Verify that the chosen API provides the apparent geocentric ecliptic longitude in the intended frame and epoch. Map normalized tropical longitude in degrees into twelve half-open 30-degree sectors beginning with Aries. Verify the library’s lunar phase convention; expose the phase angle, display phase, and the precise UTC instants for upcoming phase events. Convert instants to the viewer’s timezone for display. “Moon phase today” uses a documented instant, for example local noon, and labels events that occur later on the same date.

**Vedic Moon.** Implement Prokerala Birth Details as the initial real server adapter using its published OpenAPI contract, client credentials flow, explicit Lahiri setting, and Chandra Rasi result. Recheck endpoint version, auth, vocabulary and response schema against the current provider docs. Use sidereal Moon for this field. If the provider returns only a sign, display the sign and omit degrees. Never invent missing longitudes or reuse a provider's sidereal Sun field as a tropical Sun result. The abstraction may later support an alternative ephemeris; ship one verified path before adding vendors. No automatic external fallback that shares birth details with a second company.

**Birth input.** For a manual sign selection collect no birth information. For calculation, ask only for necessary fields. Offer a bundled and licensed city/timezone lookup including Chakdaha, Kalyani and Kolkata with checked coordinates, plus manual coordinates and an IANA timezone for unlisted places. Show the selected town, country and timezone for confirmation; distinguish places sharing a name. Request neither contacts nor precise device location. Interpret the local civil birth time using historical IANA timezone rules. Handle nonexistent local times and ask the user which UTC offset applies when a clock change creates two possible instants. Before a vendor call name the provider and show exactly which inputs will leave the device. Never send a person's name or writing. Delete raw input after calculation unless explicitly saved to the encrypted vault.

**Uncertainty.** “I don’t know the time” is a first-class choice. Determine the possible interval for the actual local civil date, including clock changes. Return a single date-only sign only if the engine can verify it stayed stable across that interval using documented ingress search or a validated equivalent. When the provider cannot determine stability, ask for a time or show “Moon sign undetermined.” When a verified interval spans a transition, show both candidates and explain that the birth time matters. Treat approximate time as a range too. Do not assume noon or derive a natal sign from today’s Moon. Restrict supported years to those validated for the engine, timezone data, and provider.

**One validated fact packet.** Each calculation returns a typed result with schema version, calculation kind, status (calculated, ambiguous, unavailable, manually selected), source and version where available, body, zodiac system, applicable ayanamsa, precision, UTC instant or interval, and only the fields supported by the source. Use language-neutral sign IDs. Translate at the UI. Publish the method in a “How this was found” drawer. Keep the astronomical result in a distinct labeled area above the creative writing invitation.

**MCP.** Implement a small server-side Model Context Protocol adapter using the official SDK. A tested local stdio transport is sufficient for the integration. It and the callable API import the same schema-checked calculation services. Expose read-only tools such as get_moon_phase, get_sign_reference and explain_fact_packet. Make the last tool return curated definitions and allowed fact fields. Never give an AI tool access to the vault, recovery material, account operations, billing, arbitrary URLs, or raw personal profiles. The normal user journey calls the backend directly and still works if the MCP client is absent. Supply a contract test that negotiates the transport, lists the tools and calls one with a synthetic date. If offering network MCP later, require service authentication, restricted scopes and an explicit allowlist.

**Cache and cost.** Cache public Moon results by calculation version, instant/date rule and timezone. Cache access tokens server-side to shortly before expiry. Private chart results remain in memory for the request or in the owner’s encrypted vault by explicit choice. Avoid persistent caches keyed by exact birth data. Validate inputs before provider calls, coalesce duplicate requests, set short timeouts, honor Retry-After and impose bounded retries and a circuit breaker. Do not multiply paid calculations by four when four UI languages use the same result. Distinguish live, cached, synthetic fixture, and unavailable states in diagnostics. Never display a fixture as a real birth result.

## 7 · Backend operations and data ownership

Implement these as typed operations. The server obtains the UID from verified authentication. Apply schema validation and authorization before accessing data or calling a paid service. Use Firebase anonymous sessions only if a guest explicitly requests an online feature requiring identity. Explain account linking and handle collisions without merging archives.

| Operation | Contract and real outcome |
|---|---|
| Read almanac | A bounded date and timezone yield deterministic public Moon facts with provenance. Local calculation handles ordinary browsing. |
| Calculate birth signs | Accept selected system, required birth fields, uncertainty and disclosure choice. Check quota and timezone. Return independent results per system, so a Vedic provider outage can leave the tropical result usable. |
| Save or sync | Encrypt the body and sensitive metadata before upload. Use a stable random item ID and a revision precondition. Concurrent edits preserve both versions or open a conflict resolution flow. Show “Backed up” after server acknowledgement. |
| List, read, remove | Owner-scoped paginated Firestore paths expose envelopes and minimal sync metadata. Keep failed writes retryable. A tombstone or equivalent revision protocol prevents stale clients from resurrecting released content. |
| Generate creative text | Accept a selected passage or public sign prompt, language, mode, consent and request ID. Verify attestation, access and quota. Return creative text, support, unavailable or rate limited. Generation runs on explicit request. |
| Export and erase | Generate encrypted portable backups locally and test import. Readable exports need a privacy preview. Account erase requires recent authentication and starts a retryable server job across user documents and Auth. Finish partial jobs independently of an already-deleted identity. Keep local and cloud erasure choices distinct. |

Use IndexedDB per vault/account for encrypted drafts, cards, letters, saved results, check-ins, preferences and pending sync operations. Encrypt sensitive queue payloads. Firestore path users/{uid}/items/{itemId} holds bounded ciphertext, envelope version, nonce, revision and server timestamps. Encrypt recipients, moods, birth facts, sign associations and text. Keep cloud documents within service limits and surface size errors before upload. Store quota reservations, expiring idempotency records, deletion jobs and aggregate counters in server-only collections with defined retention. Bundle the public sign catalog and translations. A public sky cache may hold only public sky data. Device-generated PNGs require no cloud bucket; default-deny any provisioned but unused storage service.

## 8 · Protect private writing throughout its lifecycle

1. **Threat model.** Document a shared phone, accidental sharing, account switches, a lost device, stale offline clients, unauthorized readers, third-party processing and a leaked recovery code. Put truthful short privacy copy beside each relevant action. Address the limits of an unlocked or compromised device.
2. **Encryption and restoration.** Use Web Crypto or a maintained audited library, cryptographically random per-vault keys, authenticated encryption such as AES-256-GCM, fresh nonces and associated data binding vault, item and schema version. Define envelope migration. For sync, wrap the data key with a high-entropy user-controlled recovery secret. Confirm recovery before the first backup. On a second device, sign-in plus the recovery secret restores the archive. Keep raw keys and recovery secrets out of the server, logs, analytics, localStorage and URLs. A convenience lock may use a supported device credential or strong passphrase-backed unlock with a documented KDF. Identify the limits of a short UI PIN and explain the consequences of losing a recovery secret.
3. **Sessions.** Namespace vaults and queues by account. On sign-out, timeout and account change, clear decrypted previews, search indexes, listeners and memory caches. Cancel stale fetches and pending uploads for the departed account. Provide a privacy cover when backgrounded where supported. Attach guest items to an account only after explicit selection.
4. **Access controls.** Default-deny Firebase rules. Enforce owner UID, fields, types, sizes, valid revisions, allowed mutations and bounded list queries. Deny client writes to quotas, entitlements, reference collections and deletion jobs. Check authorization inside Admin SDK functions. Enforce App Check on sensitive production cloud paths. Use least-privilege service identities and restricted provider credentials.
5. **Browser and logs.** HTTPS, narrow trusted origins, restrictive CSP compatible with the configured Firebase flow, safe text rendering, dependency scanning and provider host allowlists. No session replay, ad pixels, raw content analytics, arbitrary model-generated HTML or third-party scripts in the writer. Cache only approved public assets in the service worker. Private responses use no-store. Inspect automatic SDK and cloud logs for URL parameters, birth information, identifiers, excerpts and credentials. Do not log provider request bodies, full errors or birth-query URLs.
6. **Abuse and spend.** Set transactional per-user and per-project daily limits, concurrency and output-token limits, timeouts, server-enforced switches and duplicate-request protection. Handle quota reservations atomically. If a paid request times out after dispatch and billing outcome is unknown, do not silently submit it again. Keep local writing and exporting available.
7. **Deletion.** Distinguish removal from active storage, pending sync, configured backup expiry and previously shared files. Define retention, backup access and restore procedures. A restore must not silently bring back deleted content. Test with synthetic data and report the actual verified time windows.

## 9 · Use Gemini where it helps the writer

Implement three explicit actions: **Hear a quiet reflection**, **Turn these lines into a poem**, and **Give me another sign-inspired question**. Return two or three short sentences, four editable lines, or one open question respectively. Preserve language and code-switching. Show the selected passage and destination before sending private words. Saving and deleting a letter must work when the writer declines AI.

Use a server-only Google-supported Gemini integration with a configured and tested model. Google’s unpaid Gemini API terms restrict submission of sensitive, confidential or personal information. For unpaid development use synthetic fixtures and keep real private writing on the device. Enable private-passage processing only under suitable service terms and an explicitly configured production policy. Enforce this on the server. Provide local editorial alternatives labeled “Writing spark” or “Use my words.”

Keep sign generation and private reflections separate. For a sign question pass only a minimized validated fact packet and an editorial theme. Render factual fields from the packet in code. For a reflection, the passage is data. The model has no tools or account access and cannot send, store or release letters. Validate structured output and handle invalid JSON, unknown fields, blocked content, excessive length and provider refusal. Do not invent chart positions or predictions about love, illness, money or death.

Suggested reflection instruction: “Write gently in the writer's language. Reflect a feeling actually present in the selected passage. Use tentative wording when the feeling is ambiguous. Avoid diagnosis, advice, prophecy and invented history. If serious distress makes a creative response inappropriate, return the support state.”

Make “Find human support” accessible by choice. Combine local language-aware detection with safeguards on server requests that already have consent. Test native scripts, transliterations and benign figurative language. For serious distress suppress generated poems/readings, preserve saving and deletion, and show a calm support card with a verified region-appropriate human contact. Maintain a regional resource file with source and verification date; verify the Indian resource before release. Always allow contacting a trusted person manually. Never transmit a letter solely to classify it or automatically contact anyone. Avoid claiming perfect detection.

## 12 · Deliver the build

Work in runnable slices: (1) local write → seal → reopen/export, (2) sign library → daily activity → letter or card, (3) deterministic astronomy and real provider adapter, (4) protected Firebase sync, recovery and erasure, (5) Gemini and MCP, (6) mobile accessibility, localization, monitoring and release checks. Complete all six. Keep preview runnable throughout. Synthetic data belongs in clearly marked tests and demo mode.

Return the working preview, source tree, exact development/test/build/deploy commands, data-flow diagram, integration configuration table and actual test results.
